About

Most of what I make is about communication beyond words: pictures that explain a feeling to a child, calming steps a teenager can follow alone, and paintings about the ways people show love without saying it.

I am a senior at Northern Valley Regional High School at Old Tappan. This year I am taking AP Research, AP Biology, AP Literature, and AP Calculus, after AP Chemistry, AP Physics 1, AP Language, AP World History, and AP Precalculus as a junior. The College Board named me an AP Scholar with Distinction in 2026. I scored a 1500 on the SAT, with an 800 in Math, and I was named a Commended Student in the 2027 National Merit Scholarship Program.

Outside class I illustrated Mia’s Feelings Lab, a seven-book children’s series that explains the science of emotions; I paint, draw, and build small sculptures; I write essays about how culture shapes the way we see ourselves; and for two years I worked shifts as a junior member of Old Tappan Volunteer Emergency Medical Services. I plan to study communication sciences and disorders, with linguistics close behind: how children learn to speak, hear, and understand, and what happens when that is hard.

Mia’s Feelings Lab

A seven-book children’s series on the science of emotions, written by Kayla Park and illustrated by me, for readers ages 7 to 13.

2026 · all seven books on Amazon in paperback and Kindle · donated to six Bergen County public libraries

Each book takes one feeling and pairs a story with a hands-on Feelings Lab activity. In Book 1, Why Do I Get So Angry?, Mia learns that a small part of the brain works like a smoke detector. In Book 2, Why Am I Shy Around New People?, she finds her way back to the lunch table one small step at a time. I drew Mia, her neighbor Dr. Reena, and every scene, keeping the pictures clear enough that a seven-year-old can read the feeling on Mia’s face before reading the words.

VOY (Voices of the Young), Wellness Content Lead

2024 to 2027, grades 10 to 12

Mo Soop (모두의 대나무 숲) is VOY’s anonymous peer-support app, “a safe space to share and heal.” I built its Exercise section: nine ways to settle your body when a feeling gets too big, each matched to a moment. Box breathing for when you feel a panic coming, a slow staircase walk for when you need a reset, a pillow to punch for when you are angry, a 20-minute walk for when your mind is racing. To write it, I studied Psychological First Aid, the method first responders use to steady people right after something frightening happens, and turned what I learned into steps a teenager can follow alone on a phone. I also draw the illustrated posts for VOY’s Instagram that introduce the app and share the same techniques.
